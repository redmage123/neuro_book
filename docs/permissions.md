# Rights and Permissions Log: How the Brain Learns

This is a working log of third-party material in *How the Brain Learns* (front matter and Chapters 0 to 16), prepared so that the author and publisher can decide what needs a permission request before production. It is not legal advice. Copyright terms below are summarized roughly: in the United States, works published before 1931 are in the public domain, and works first published abroad between 1929 and 1977 generally run 95 years from publication (restored under the URAA where formalities were missed); in the EU and UK, a work is generally protected until 70 years after the end of the year in which the author (or, for a translation, the translator) died. Dates marked "verify" have not been confirmed against a primary source. Word counts exclude the attribution line. Paths are relative to `docs/book/Word/`.

A general note on epigraphs: publishers commonly treat an epigraph from a copyrighted work as decorative rather than as quotation for criticism or comment, so many do not rely on fair use for epigraphs, however short. The recommendations below follow that conservative practice.

## 3. Figures

Every figure was checked against its chapter's "Figure Attribution" section and the chapter's `make_figures.py`. Figures "drawn for this book" are original artwork. Where a drawn figure plots numbers or reproduces a functional form from a published paper, the underlying data and equations are not copyrightable; only a close copy of the published figure's visual design would need permission. Credit lines for these should read "Data from" or "After", as most already do.
