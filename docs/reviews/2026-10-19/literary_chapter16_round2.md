## Overall assessment

The ball is beside the eye" (line 12), with the honest correction about which checkpoint gave which result. Then: "Athena was built from the principles of this book, by its author, and this chapter asks why it does not yet work." It is as strong an opening as any in Part VI, and it sets the chapter's register in a sentence: candid, specific, and curious rather than defensive.
- **The ending returns to the book.** The microprocessor mirror (line 118) closes the frame Chapter 15 opened: "a brain whose every line of code is known, which its builder could not understand or repair without one". The Box epigraph is paid off, and Cajal's sculptor gets the last image. The book's final sentence now looks back over the whole book rather than at one project.
- **The audit register is gone.** The receptor list is a single clause (line 24). The constant-surprise detail is glossed in plain words (line 56). The test-suite sentence is cut, and "killed dozens of times" (line 76) will not date the book to a day.
- **The expert's corrections strengthen the diagnosis.** The output collapse is now described as a readout problem, "some 15,000 spikes in each time step" swamping "the handful of neurons that carry the prompt" (line 86). That is more vivid and more accurate than the earlier credit-assignment reading. It also connects to Chapter 6's gain control, and credit assignment now stands as a separate and clearly stated limit (line 88). "Scale is probably not the main obstacle; organization is" (line 92) is properly hedged without losing its force.
- **The tone holds.** The author's public writing is attributed (line 78). The moving-target sentence (line 18) is graceful. The third-person disclosure is consistent with the book; I accept the coordinator's reason for keeping it.
- **The house style holds,** with no em dashes, numerals per the house rule, and "well-being".

Prose quality: 5/5. Reader interest: 5/5.

### Copyedits (optional; do not affect the score)

- **Lines 20 and 116 (recommended).** "a system that fails in the places the theory predicts is evidence that the theory has found what matters" (line 116), and "the failures, in turn, are evidence for the book" (line 20). The failures were read through the book after they happened, not predicted in advance, so "predicts" claims slightly more than the chapter has shown. In a chapter whose authority rests on exactness about its own claims, the weaker wording is the stronger one.
  - Line 116: use "a system that fails where the theory says the hard parts are is evidence that the theory has found what matters".
  - Line 20: use "and the failures, in turn, are consistent with the book's account of what is hard".
- **Lines 12 and 16.** Athena is introduced in line 12 and then again in line 16 ("Its trained instance is called Athena"). Reorder line 16's opening: "Athena is the trained instance of NIMCP, short for Neuro-Inspired Modular Control Protocol, an experimental software system that simulates the machinery this book has described and tries to raise it like a child."
- **Line 42.** "an early, small version of the spiking network, with 768 neurons trained by a variant of backpropagation rather than by the biological rules" is the expert's useful correction, but it makes the sentence long. Consider a full stop after "rules": "It settled into firing about 26 times a second ...".

Counts: Major 0, Minor 0, Copyedit 3.
