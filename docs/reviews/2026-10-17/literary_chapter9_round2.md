## Findings

### Major

None.

### Minor

- **Line 12.** "This chapter shows what it achieves, because the same process ..." The "because" is weak and makes the reader look for a causal link. Use a colon: "This chapter shows what it achieves: the same process that makes a habit blind to its outcome is the one that lets a pianist ..."
- **Line 18.** The second sentence of the thesis carries three claims and about 50 words. Split it: "It binds separate actions into units and hands their control from circuits that deliberate to circuits that execute, through the basal ganglia and the dopamine signal of Chapter 3. The same dopamine signal grades the small variations that practice produces and keeps the ones that work."
- **Line 42 (206 words, two topics).** Move the Taylor sentence ("Jordan Taylor, Krakauer, and Richard Ivry later asked ...") to open line 44. Its fast and slow split is the natural lead-in to "Adaptation has a memory with more than one timescale." Line 42 then ends on the Mazzoni punchline.
- **Line 44 (savings sentence).** "The same model explains some of the savings by which a perturbation met a second time is relearned faster, but not savings after the first learning has been fully washed out, which appear to depend partly on remembering the explicit aiming strategy that worked before." This sentence has to be read twice. Rewrite: "The model also explains part of savings, the faster relearning of a perturbation met a second time. When the first learning has been fully washed out, however, savings seem to depend on something else: remembering the aiming strategy that worked before."
- **Line 48 (two topics).** The paragraph covers other teachers of adaptation and then the adaptation-versus-skill turn. Split before "Adaptation, however learned, is recalibration". The section then ends on its own short paragraph that hands over to the rest of the chapter.
- **Line 58.** "while frontoparietal cortex was more active when a sequence was being broken into them" The term "frontoparietal" is undefined, and "broken into them" makes the reader look back to find "chunks". Rewrite: "while regions of frontal and parietal cortex were more active when a sequence was being divided into chunks."
- **Line 64.** "The two output pathways of the striatum described in Chapter 4 appear to divide the work, one more involved in the elements within a sequence and the other in marking where a sequence begins and ends." The reader is not told which pathway does which, yet line 68 then names "the indirect pathway" as if it had been introduced. Name them here, as "the direct pathway ... the indirect pathway" in whichever order the expert confirms for Jin, Tecuapetla, and Costa (2014).
- **Line 70 (two topics; "handover" twice).** "Dopamine is needed for the handover ... The anatomy has a route for the handover." Split before "Dopamine is needed". In the second paragraph, change "The anatomy has a route for the handover" to "The anatomy shows how dopamine could pass the handover along."
- **Line 94 (one-sentence paragraph).** The Elbert sentence was left alone when Draganski was cut. Move "Long practice leaves a lasting mark" and the Elbert sentence to the end of line 92. Then split line 92 before "Alvaro Pascual-Leone", so that Nudo stands alone and Pascual-Leone and Elbert form the human paragraph. Line 142's "the plasticity that enlarges a string player's finger map" still finds its antecedent.
- **Lines 116 and 118 (split in the wrong place).** Masters belongs with choking, but the split put him at the head of the Poldrack paragraph. Move the Masters sentence to the end of line 116, so that line 118 begins "In the brain, automaticity looks less like a move to a new region ..."
- **Line 130.** "tested children aged eight and 12 throwing beanbags, without seeing the target as they threw, at a target on the floor" The word "target" appears twice, and the sentence holds its main verb too long. "Before each test, one group warmed up at the test distance" also leaves the reader unsure whether the groups differed in a whole program of practice or only in a warm-up. The answer decides whether "practiced around it" (line 154) is earned. Rewrite: "Robert Kerr and Bernard Booth had children aged eight and 12 throw beanbags at a target on the floor without seeing it as they threw." Then state the practice difference in one plain sentence, with the expert's wording on what the groups practiced.
- **Line 136 (three topics).** The paragraph runs popcorn, then the failed laboratory habits, then Wood's lever. Move "The time scale matters too. As Chapter 8 noted ... over months." to the end of line 134, where it follows Lally's months-long curves. Write "timescale" as one word, as at lines 44 and 104. The popcorn then leads straight to "Habits are cued by context".
- **Line 142 (183 words).** The Fleisher sentence beginning "For three decades" runs about 70 words through a semicolon. Split it: "For three decades he played only music written for the left hand, and taught and conducted. In the 1990s, after the condition had been identified as focal dystonia and treated with injections of botulinum toxin and a deep-tissue massage called Rolfing, he began to play with both hands again, and in 2004 he recorded his first two-handed album in four decades." Then start a new paragraph at "About one in 100 professional musicians".
- **Line 156.** The fourth point mixes brain and machine within one semicolon sentence, and it opens with a caveat before the claim has been made. Rewrite: "Fourth, the brain largely adds skills without erasing old ones. A network learning a new skill tends to overwrite the ones it has, the catastrophic interference of Chapter 5, and methods that protect important weights, such as James Kirkpatrick and colleagues' elastic weight consolidation, are partial remedies. Motor cortex instead forms new spines for a new skill and keeps a few of them for life while leaving the old ones in place, although two conflicting adaptations learned close together do interfere."
- **Line 162.** "The brain pays that price because it never lets the slow system go" This is my own round-1 wording, and it is a non sequitur. Keeping the slow system is why the price is affordable, not why it is paid. Use: "The brain can afford that price because it never lets the slow system go: ..."
