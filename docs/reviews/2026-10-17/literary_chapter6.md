# Literary Review: Chapter 6, "Perception as Inference: How the Brain Sees What It Expects"

Source: `docs/book/Word/Chapter6/How_the_Brain_Learns_Chapter_6_Perception_as_Inference.md` (458 lines; body ends at line 162, about 6,200 words of body text, 10 figures)

## Overall assessment

What works: the opening question, "When you open your eyes, why do you see a world rather than a pattern of light?" (line 12), is the best hook in the book, and the chapter answers it. "Bayes in One Paragraph" really is one paragraph and is correct. Nearly every claim is attached to something the reader can try or has seen: the blind spot, the eyelid press, the hollow mask, the checker-shadow squares, the gorilla, the ventriloquist. The chapter braids every earlier chapter back in without strain (retinal compression from Chapter 4, acetylcholine and norepinephrine from Chapter 3, priors as synaptic weights and the two consolidation clocks from Chapter 5, the cerebellar forward model from Chapter 4), so the book ends its first six chapters feeling like one argument. The AI section is the most substantive of the five, and the "When Inference Goes Wrong" section is careful to label its clinical accounts as hypotheses. The closing sentence lands.

What does not work: local matters only. Three paragraphs run to 150-200 words and should be split; a figure is referenced eighty lines before it appears; "prediction error" is introduced without distinguishing it from Chapter 3's dopamine prediction error, a collision this readership will notice; a sentence is attributed to Chapter 1 that Chapter 1 does not contain; one caption carries an inline source unlike every other caption in Chapters 1-5; "grey" and "cancelled" are British in an American-spelled book; one superlative cannot be sourced. Prose quality: 5/5. Reader interest: 5/5.

## Findings

### Major (must fix before publication)

- **Line 112.** "Chapter 1 said that the brain generates and the world corrects, and the account so far has treated the brain as if it sat still" Chapter 1 contains no such sentence (Chapter 4 line 80 makes the same attribution). Add the sentence to Chapter 1 (see that report), or rewrite here: "Chapter 1 framed the brain as a controller, something that generates and lets the world correct, and the account so far..."
- **Lines 62-68.** "sends upward only the difference, the prediction error." The term is introduced for sensory predictive coding with no acknowledgment that Chapter 3 used "prediction error" for the scalar dopamine reward signal. Readers who know temporal-difference learning will assume they are the same thing. Add after line 62: "This is prediction error in a different sense from Chapter 3. There, a single number, broadcast by dopamine, said how much better or worse than expected an outcome was. Here, a whole pattern of sensory errors passes between cortical layers. The two are cousins, and theorists have tried to unify them, but they are not the same signal."

### Minor (should fix)

- **Line 14.** "A trapezoid on the retina can be a trapezoid face on" Hyphenate: "face-on".
- **Lines 14, 76, 78.** "A patch of grey" / "exactly the same grey" / "the same shade of grey" British spelling; Chapter 5 line 140 uses "gray". Standardize to "gray".
- **Line 24.** "Estimates of the information rate of the optic nerve are on the order of ten megabits per second, less than a single compressed video stream." A compressed 1080p stream runs 5 to 10 megabits per second, so "less than" is shaky. "comparable to a single compressed video stream".
- **Line 52.** "A V1 neuron responds to an oriented edge at one place (Figure 6.10)." Figure 6.10 appears at line 130, seventy-eight lines later, in a different section. Either move the figure here and reference it again at line 128, or drop this parenthetical.
- **Line 68.** The paragraph from "All three are observed" to "a line that stops is not" is 150 words with four pieces of evidence. Split before "And neurons in V1 respond to a line..." Also, "it is the single most reproducible phenomenon in sensory cortex" is a superlative that cannot be sourced; "one of the most reproducible phenomena in sensory cortex".
- **Line 78 (caption).** "(Source: Edward H. Adelson, vectorized by Wikimedia Commons user Pbroks13, CC BY-SA 4.0.)" The only caption in Chapters 1-6 with an inline source; the Figure Attribution section (line 170) already carries it. Remove from the caption.
- **Line 84.** "the fins are read as the edges of corners in a carpentered world" "Carpentered world" is a term of art. "in a world of right-angled rooms and buildings, what Gregory called a carpentered world".
- **Line 114.** The paragraph from "The first is that the brain must distinguish" to "cancelled before it is felt" is about 200 words. Split after "and the comparison is shown in Figure 6.9." and again before "The same mechanism is why you cannot tickle yourself".
- **Lines 114 and 116.** "the sensation is predicted and cancelled before it is felt" / "self-caused changes are cancelled" British double-l; "canceled" (see Chapter 4 line 36).
- **Line 128.** "The units it learned were localized, oriented, band-pass filters, Figure 6.10, essentially the receptive fields that Hubel and Wiesel had found" The figure reference set off by commas reads as an item in the list. "band-pass filters (Figure 6.10), essentially the receptive fields..."
- **Line 132.** "The adult perceptual system is a model under continual revision, slow in its deep layers and fast at its edges" "Deep layers" was used at line 70 for cortical layers 5 and 6; here it means something else. "slow at its core and fast at its periphery".
- **Line 132.** "Wear goggles that invert the world and within days it begins to look upright again." Whether the world "looks" upright after Stratton's and Kohler's goggles is contested; what is agreed is behavioral adaptation. "and within days you begin to move through it as if it were upright."
- **Line 140.** "the weights are set by mechanisms, inhibition, acetylcholine, dopamine, and oscillatory gain, that are known and can be measured." The appositive list inside commas cannot be parsed on first reading. "the weights are set by mechanisms that are known and can be measured: inhibition, acetylcholine, dopamine, and oscillatory gain." Also, dopamine's role in precision has not been established in this chapter (only acetylcholine and norepinephrine, line 96); either add a clause there or drop "dopamine" here.

### Nit (optional)

- **Line 6.** The Southall translation of the epigraph is awkward ("Such objects are always imagined as being present in the field of vision as would have to be there...") but authentic. Acceptable; the paraphrase at line 18 does the work.
- **Line 26.** "a dozen times the width of the full moon" Correct (the moon subtends about half a degree) and vivid; keep.
- **Line 42.** "and turning the page swaps them (Figure 6.3)" Panel B already shows the rotated image, so "turning the page" is redundant in print and meaningless on a screen. "and rotating the image swaps them (Figure 6.3B)".
- **Line 104.** "In 2002 Marc Ernst and Martin Banks did this experiment" "did" → "ran".
- **Line 138.** "the condition is named for Charles Bonnet, who described it in his grandfather in 1760" Good detail; keep.
- **Line 162.** "Chapter 7: Emotion and the Body --- How Internal States Tell the Brain What Matters" Chapter 7 was not supplied for review; confirm it exists with this subtitle.

Counts: Major 2, Minor 13, Nit 6.
