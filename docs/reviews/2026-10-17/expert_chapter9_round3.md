## Minor (optional, does not affect the score)

- **Line 162.** "Motor cortex instead forms new spines ... although two conflicting adaptations learned close together do interfere.[^96]" The footnote cites only Brashers-Krug et al. (1996). That study is about the interference, which concerns force-field adaptation rather than motor cortex spines. Read quickly, the sentence seems to attribute interference to the spine findings. A clearer version would split it: "Motor cortex instead forms new spines for a new skill and keeps a few of them for life while leaving the old ones in place. Not all motor memories coexist so easily: two conflicting adaptations learned close together do interfere.[^96]" If you want the spine claim footnoted here as well, add Xu et al. 2009 and Yang et al. 2009, which the chapter already cites.
- **Line 44.** The Taylor, Krakauer and Ivry sentence now opens the paragraph about timescales, so the paragraph begins on explicit strategy and then turns to spontaneous recovery. This is technically correct. As an editorial point, Taylor sits more naturally at the end of the Mazzoni paragraph, as it did before.
