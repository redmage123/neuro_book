# Literary Review: "How the Brain Learns", Chapters 0-6, Book-Level Summary

Reviewed: the seven chapter sources under `docs/book/Word/Chapter0` through `Chapter6`. Per-chapter reports are in `literary_chapter0.md` through `literary_chapter6.md` alongside this file. Reference lists and footnote text were ignored except where they affect the body.

## Overall

This is two books stapled together, and the seam is between Chapter 3 and Chapter 4. Chapters 4-6 are a mature popular-science narrative: 4,500 to 6,500 words of body text each, an experiment or a number in nearly every paragraph, figures the text talks about, aphorisms that earn their place, and a fixed architecture (opening section that picks up the previous chapter's promise, body, "Implications for Artificial Intelligence", "The Central Lesson", "Next Chapter"). They would publish with light copyediting. Chapters 0-3 are a primer and three position essays: 1,300 to 2,900 words of body each, many sections of one or two sentences, almost no numbers or named experiments in the text, figures that are frequently never mentioned, and in Chapter 0 a textbook apparatus (bold terms, bullets, summary table, glossary) that nothing else in the book uses. The stance is consistent throughout (anti-computer-metaphor, pro-constraint, pro-"the brain solves a different problem"), the sequence is right, and the later chapters pay off the earlier ones' promises with real care. But a reader who judges the book by its first three chapters will undervalue it, and the later chapters repeatedly cite the earlier ones for content they do not actually contain.

Is the length disparity a problem? Yes. It is not that Chapters 4-6 are too long; they are the right length for their material. It is that Chapters 1-3 are outlines of chapters at the standard the book later sets. The fix is to bring each of them to roughly 4,000-5,000 words of body using the recipe Chapters 4-6 already follow: one named experiment per section, one number per claim, every figure referenced from the text, and an "Implications for Artificial Intelligence" section and a "Central Lesson" paragraph in each (Chapter 3 has these; Chapters 1 and 2 do not). The per-chapter reports list the specific sections to expand and, in most cases, the specific experiment from the chapter's own references to expand them with (Schultz's juice experiment for Chapter 3; Adrian's rate code and the 2-percent/20-percent energy figures for Chapter 2; the 20-watt budget and synaptic failure rate for Chapter 1).

## Structure and flow

- **Sequence.** 1 (frame) → 2 (neuron) → 3 (chemical context) → 4 (circuits) → 5 (plasticity) → 6 (perception) flows well; each chapter opens by naming what the previous one left undone, and Chapter 5's "Every chapter so far has ended at the same door" and Chapter 6's "Every chapter since has built the machinery that makes the sentence true" are exactly how a book should feel at its midpoint.
- **Chapter 0's status is undecided and the book shows it.** Chapter 1 opens with "This book is about the brain, but it is not written primarily for neuroscientists," never mentions Chapter 0, and says it "has deliberately avoided detailed neurobiology"; Chapter 5's roll-call starts at Chapter 1. Chapter 0 is correct and useful but written in a different register. Recommendation: make it an appendix ("A Primer on Cells and Molecules"), cut the emergence/consciousness section, strip the textbook apparatus, and have Chapters 1 and 2 point to it. If it stays as Chapter 0, the same edits apply and Chapter 1's opening must acknowledge it.
- **"Next Chapter" teasers** match the actual subtitles in every case but one: Chapter 2 announces "Chapter 3 --- Neuromodulation: How Chemical Context Shapes Neural Behavior" but Chapter 3 is subtitled "...Shapes Learning and Behavior". Chapter 0 formats its teaser differently (bold plus a sentence) from all the others (a plain line). Chapter 2 buries its teaser inside a "Looking Ahead" section and then ends on a figure.
- **Promises kept.** Chapter 1's "learning is slow because molecular change is slow" (paid in Chapter 5); Chapter 1's "perception is an active process of inference" (paid in Chapter 6); Chapter 4's deferral of hippocampal plasticity (paid in Chapter 5); Chapter 3's eligibility trace (paid as synaptic tagging in Chapter 5 and as the cerebellar climbing fiber in Chapter 4).
- **Promises not kept.** (a) Chapter 4 line 80 and Chapter 6 line 112 both quote Chapter 1 for "the controller generates, the world corrects"; Chapter 1 contains no such sentence. (b) Chapter 5 line 90 says "Chapter 3 described a three-factor rule"; the term appears in Chapter 3 only in a figure caption. (c) Chapter 6 introduces "prediction error" for predictive coding without distinguishing it from Chapter 3's dopamine reward prediction error, a collision this readership (who know TD error) will notice. (d) Chapter 3 asserts twice that the brain "does not optimize a loss function"; Chapter 6 then reports cue combination that "matched the computed optimum". These need one sentence each.
- **Voice.** Consistent in attitude, inconsistent in texture. Chapters 1-3 are aphoristic and staccato, with frequent one-sentence paragraphs and chains of fragments ("Synapses fail. Neurons die. Signals degrade."); Chapters 4-6 use longer periodic sentences and commas. Em-dash density goes from 19 in Chapter 0's body to zero in Chapters 4-6. The "not a bug, a feature" move appears five times (Chapter 1 twice, Chapter 4, Chapter 5 twice) and the "from an engineering perspective" opener three times. "Crucially" appears in three consecutive chapters.

## Consistency

- **Spelling.** American throughout with British strays: "nanometres" (Chapter 3 caption), "cancelled" (Chapter 4; Chapter 6 twice), "grey" (Chapter 6, three times) against "gray" (Chapter 5). Centuries are "19th century"/"20th century" in Chapter 0 but "twentieth century"/"eighteenth century" in Chapters 4 and 6. Serial comma is used everywhere except one Chapter 2 caption.
- **Epigraph attribution** differs in every chapter: life dates (1), a year (0), work and year (4, 5, 6), nothing (3). Chapter 0's epigraph is admittedly a paraphrase; Chapter 3's is admittedly unverified. Chapter 2 has no epigraph.
- **Captions.** Chapter 0 puts "(Source: ...)" in all nine captions; Chapters 1-5 put sources only in the attribution section; Chapter 6 does both for one figure. Figures never referenced from the text: 1.1, 1.2, 1.3, 3.4, 3.9, 3.10, 5.6; Figure 0.7 is in the wrong subsection; Chapter 6 references Figure 6.10 seventy-eight lines before it appears.
- **Headings.** Chapter 0 alone uses second-level subheadings, a Summary and a Glossary. Chapter 2 alone opens with "# Introduction". Chapter 3's "A Note on..." is a sidebar at heading level.
- **Source hygiene.** Chapter 3 has backslash-escaped quotes (`\"`, `\'`) at six lines; Chapter 4 cites footnote 43 before 40-42; Chapter 0 hangs a footnote marker off an image line; two figure filenames (`fig_context.png`, `fig_systems.png`) are reused across chapters for different figures (harmless while images are per-chapter folders, dangerous if they are ever merged).
- **Terminology introduced before definition:** ATP (Chapter 2 caption; defined only in Chapter 0's glossary), myelin and synaptic cleft (Chapter 2 captions only), place cells (Chapter 4, defined by implication in Chapter 5), the EEG band names (Chapter 4), temporal-difference (Chapter 3 caption), Mach bands (Chapter 4, no figure), AMPA/NMDA (Chapter 5), carpentered world (Chapter 6).

## The five most important things to improve

1. **Bring Chapters 1-3 up to the standard of 4-6.** Expand every one- or two-sentence section into a paragraph built around a named experiment and a number; add "Implications for Artificial Intelligence" and "The Central Lesson" to Chapters 1 and 2; narrate Schultz's experiment in Chapter 3; write or cut Chapter 3's "Clinical Evidence" and its two hardware figures; give Chapter 2 an epigraph and a proper ending. Target 4,000-5,000 words of body each.
2. **Decide what Chapter 0 is and make the book agree.** Appendix or Chapter 0, strip the bold/bullets/table/glossary, cut the emergence section, fix the summary-table contradiction on emergence, and make Chapter 1's opening acknowledge it (or move that opening to a preface).
3. **Repair the cross-references.** Add "the brain generates behavior, and the world corrects it" to Chapter 1; define "three-factor rule" in Chapter 3's text; fix Chapter 2's teaser subtitle; distinguish reward prediction error (Chapter 3) from sensory prediction error (Chapter 6); soften "does not optimize a loss function"; verify or replace Chapter 3's epigraph; fix Chapter 5's LTD date.
4. **Normalize conventions in one pass.** American spelling (gray, canceled, nanometers), centuries in words, one epigraph format (work and year), captions without inline sources, serial comma, unescaped quotes, sequential footnotes, unique figure filenames.
5. **Make every figure earn a sentence.** Reference each figure from the text or cut it; move Figure 0.7 and Figure 3.4 next to the text they illustrate; move or un-reference Chapter 6's early Figure 6.10 pointer; fix the one wrong technical claim in Chapter 4's AI section (weights carry sign, not unit outputs) and the false claim about engineers and redundancy in Chapter 1.
