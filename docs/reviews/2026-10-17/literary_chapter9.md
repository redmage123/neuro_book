# Literary Review: Chapter 9, "Habits, Skills, and Automaticity: How Practice Turns Choices into Routines"

Source: `docs/book/Word/Chapter9/How_the_Brain_Learns_Chapter_9_Habits_Skills_and_Automaticity.md` (body lines 10 to 166, about 7,080 words; 7 figures; 97 footnotes).

## Overall assessment

What works: the chapter picks up Chapter 8's devalued rats and turns them around, so that "a failure of flexibility" becomes "an achievement" (line 12). That is a real argument and a good hook. The house voice holds: one named experiment per claim, contested results stated as contested (lines 26, 92, 96 to 98, 116, 148), American spelling, no em dashes. The best passages are Mazzoni's aiming trick (line 44), the runner who coded digits as race times (line 58), "A habit is not only stored; it is continuously permitted" (line 78), Hayashi-Takagi erasing a skill (line 90), the child who "knows that the answer is twelve" (line 106), the stale popcorn (line 138), and Kerr and Booth's beanbags, paid off in the AI section (line 158). The callbacks I checked are accurate, and all seven figures are cited.

What does not work: (1) The middle third (lines 54 to 92, about 2,800 words) is a catalogue of roughly 30 studies, often three to a paragraph, with no question carrying the reader. (2) The thesis promises three mechanisms; the chapter delivers a fourth (dopamine grading variability) in a section stranded between two applied sections (line 124). (3) The AI section's AlphaGo and arbitration points (lines 154, 162) and parts of "When Routines Go Wrong" (lines 140, 148) repeat Chapter 8. (4) The Central Lesson is a section recap, and nothing hands over to Chapter 10. (5) No human being stays on the page for longer than a sentence. Prose quality: 4/5. Reader interest: 3/5.

**To reach 5/5 on prose:** fix the sentences that must be read twice (lines 32, 48, 102, 116, 154), split the paragraphs flagged below, remove the doubled Whitehead quotation and the two inaccurate "Chapter 8 ended" claims, and rewrite the Central Lesson as one lesson. **To reach 5/5 on reader interest:** make the cuts under "Length and balance" so each middle paragraph carries one story, move "Variability as Exploration" into the mechanistic arc, add one human case, and make the AI section say what this chapter adds to Chapter 8.

## Findings

### Major

- **Lines 12 and 16 (opening).** "Chapter 8 ended with rats that kept pressing a lever" and, four sentences later, "Chapter 8 ended on the same economy." Neither is accurate: the devaluation rats are in Chapter 8's "Goals and Habits", and its body ends on "habits that have outlived their reasons", a better hook. Line 16 also restates the epigraph almost word for word. **Rewrite line 12's first sentence:** "Chapter 8 closed on habits that have outlived their reasons, and on rats that kept pressing a lever for food they would no longer eat." **Rewrite line 16:** "Whitehead, who supplies this chapter's epigraph, added that operations of thought are like cavalry charges in a battle: strictly limited in number, they require fresh horses, and must be made only at decisive moments.[^2] Chapter 8 met the same economy in the cost of thinking. A brain that could not hand routine work to something cheaper would spend its limited thought on tying shoes." This saves about 25 words.

- **Lines 18 and 124 to 132 (thesis and the misplaced mechanism).** "Variability as Exploration" adds a fourth mechanism that the thesis omits, and placed after "How Practice Should Be Practiced" it interrupts the move from laboratory to life. **Fix:** move it to follow "From Planning Circuits to Doing Circuits", so the Ölveczky thread runs from rats to songbirds. Begin line 86 with "Whatever runs a finished skill, practice changes motor cortex on the way." At line 18, add to the basal ganglia sentence: "...through the basal ganglia and the dopamine signal of Chapter 3, which also grades the small variations that practice produces and keeps the ones that work."

- **Lines 54 to 92 (catalogue).** Line 68 has three studies, line 80 three Ölveczky papers, lines 86 to 92 ten studies in four paragraphs, line 110 four. The reader cannot hold them, and the strong ones lose force. Cuts are under "Length and balance". Where a study is cited in passing, name one investigator and "colleagues" (line 60: "Okihide Hikosaka and colleagues").

- **Lines 154 and 162 (AI section repeats Chapter 8).** Chapter 8 already presents AlphaGo as habit plus planning and closes "The brain's addition is arbitration." **Fix line 154:** open with "Chapter 8 described AlphaGo pairing fast networks with search. Its successor, AlphaGo Zero, went a step further and turned search into reflex: ..." **Fix line 162:** make the point specific to this chapter, interruption rather than arbitration in general. "Fifth, the brain lets the slow system interrupt the fast one. Chapter 8 described arbitration between planning and habit; practice adds a twist, because a routine runs only while a supervisor permits it, as the infralimbic experiments show. A pianist plays automatically until a wrong note ..." Line 152 is a one-sentence paragraph, which Chapter 8's review also flagged. Add: "Where Chapter 8 asked how machines choose, this section asks how they stop having to."

- **Line 166 (Central Lesson is a recap; no handover).** It walks through the circuits in section order. **Rewrite:** "Practice is how the brain spends deliberation once so that it need not spend it again. Each repetition hands a little more of an action to circuits that do not think: the cerebellum keeps it calibrated, the basal ganglia package it into units that run whole, dopamine keeps the variations that worked, and motor cortex builds and then protects the few connections that hold it. The result is Whitehead's economy, an answer remembered rather than computed, and its price is Chapter 8's rat, still pressing for food it will not eat. The brain pays that price because it never lets the slow system go: deliberation moves to the edges of a routine and stands guard, ready to take control back when the expected outcome fails to arrive. Expertise is the freedom to spend thought only where it is needed." End the fifth AI point with a handover: "Whether machines can learn this, and what else they lack, is the question of Chapter 10."

### Minor

- **Line 32.** "trace a difficult path slowly or quickly and badly" parses as both badly. Use "trace a difficult path accurately by going slowly, or quickly by accepting errors". Cut the Fitts's law sentence; it is never used again.
- **Line 36.** "Knowing that and knowing how depend on different circuits." The phrase needs its object: "Knowing that something happened and knowing how to do something depend on different circuits."
- **Line 40.** "This lasting mirror-image error" contradicts "must recalibrate back", since the error washes out. Use "This mirror-image error, called an aftereffect, ..."
- **Line 46.** Morehead repeats Mazzoni's point. Cut it; keep Taylor, who leads into Figure 9.2.
- **Line 48.** A 35-word subject ("And a person who adapts for a long time, ... drifts back"). Rewrite: "Spontaneous recovery is stranger. Adapt a person for a long time, then briefly the other way until performance is back at baseline, then withhold all error information: the person drifts back toward the first adaptation."
- **Line 58.** Split before "K. Anders Ericsson". Qualify "about seven items" with "(later estimates put the limit nearer four)"; expert to confirm (Cowan 2001).
- **Line 62.** Repeats line 36's point; cut it or fold one sentence into line 36.
- **Line 68.** "projection neurons and interneurons" are undefined, and the Martiros sentence adds nothing the reader needs. Cut it.
- **Line 78.** "keeps a hand in" is a near-cliché. Use "Cortex does not let go of a habit even after the striatum has taken it over."
- **Lines 88, 90, 110, 116 (paragraphs of 173 to 194 words).** Split line 88 before "Long practice leaves a lasting mark", line 110 before "Richard Masters", and line 116 before "The claim has been tested". Line 90 can stand, because it builds to a causal finale.
- **Line 98.** "magnetoencephalography" Add "the magnetic recording of Chapter 8".
- **Line 102.** The last sentence chains three "and" clauses; end it after "consistent" and add "Once developed, it is hard to suppress." Quote James directly: "habit diminishes the conscious attention with which our acts are performed."
- **Line 110.** Wulf's finding is practice advice; move it after Kerr and Booth (line 122). Define "choking, the collapse of a practiced skill under pressure".
- **Line 116.** Name the hook: this is the study Malcolm Gladwell popularized as the "10,000-hour rule". "did not replicate ...; the effect was substantial but considerably smaller" reads as a contradiction. Use "Practice still predicted skill, but much less strongly, and it did not separate the best violinists from the merely good" (expert to confirm).
- **Line 140.** A one-sentence paragraph repeating Chapter 8's de Wit result. Fold into line 138.
- **Line 146 (a human case).** The only lasting human presence is H.M., borrowed from Chapter 5. Add three sentences on the pianist Leon Fleisher, who lost control of his right hand to dystonia in the 1960s and returned to two-handed playing decades later (expert to verify dates).
- **Line 148.** The closing "loss of the balance between routine and deliberation" does not fit dystonia. Use "In each of these disorders the machinery of routine works too well, too little, or in the wrong place." Cut the Gillan sentence, which Chapter 8 already covers.
- **Line 154.** "trained in the same way to give, without writing out their reasoning, the answers that step-by-step reasoning once produced" Rewrite: "trained the same way: answers first reached by step-by-step reasoning become targets, and the model learns to give them directly." Line 156: replace "distilled from search" with "trained to copy a search".
- **Figure 9.4.** The caption and line 74 describe a spiral from the ventral striatum, but the diagram shows no spiral and no ventral striatum. Draw the spiral arrows or drop "spiral".
- **Figure 9.7.** HVC, RA, VTA, and DLM are never named in the text; add "(the nuclei are known by their abbreviations)" to the caption, and relabel "performance error" as "better or worse than expected" to match line 132.

## Length and balance

The body is about 7,080 words, just above the 6,500 to 7,000 target; the real problem is density. "Chunking" (780), "Recalibration" (710), "From Planning Circuits" (670), and "The Cortex Rewires" (610) hold the mechanisms and most of the catalogue. "Learning Between Sessions" (290) is short but earns its place as the clearest claim-and-rebuttal in the chapter.

Cuts (about 480 words): line 16 restated epigraph (25), line 32 Fitts's law (50), line 46 Morehead (50), line 62 folded into line 36 (70), line 68 Martiros (40), line 80 Wolff (35), line 86 Karni, whom Pascual-Leone makes redundant (55), line 92 Peters (40), line 140 merged (25), line 148 Gillan (45), and optionally line 88 Draganski (45). Additions (about 170): Fleisher (60), the thesis clause (25), the AI framing and Chapter 10 handover (45), the Gladwell clause (15), the Chapter 8 acknowledgments (25). The result is about 6,750 words, with roughly 20 studies in the middle third instead of 30.
