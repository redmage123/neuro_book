## Moderate (misleading or outdated, should fix)

- **Line 106**: "The amygdala is needed for fear of external threats, the kind learned from experience. A threat from inside the body ... produces fear without it."
  - **What is wrong**: The cited studies contrast external with internal threats, not learned with unlearned ones. Feinstein et al. 2013 draw "an important distinction between fear triggered by external threats from the environment versus fear triggered internally by CO2". The external threats S.M. failed to fear in Feinstein et al. 2011 were live snakes and spiders, a haunted house and horror films. Those are largely unlearned or culturally given, not conditioned. "The kind learned from experience" therefore overstates what the S.M. data show.
  - **Suggested rewrite**: "The amygdala is needed for fear of threats in the outside world. A threat from inside the body, detected by chemical sensors elsewhere, presumably in the brainstem, produces fear without it."

- **Line 124**: "The settings of this system are themselves learned. ... The settings are also shaped by company: women ... showed less activity in threat-related regions while holding their husband's hand. The stress system's predictions, like perception's priors, are written by experience."
  - **The maternal-care effect is not learning**. Liu et al. 1997 describe it as developmental "programming" of the HPA axis in the first ten days of life, later traced to epigenetic regulation of the glucocorticoid receptor gene (Weaver et al. 2004, Nat. Neurosci. 7:847).
  - **Coan et al. measured something else**. Their study measured an acute, moment-to-moment reduction in the neural response to threat, not a change in the system's settings.
  - **Why it matters**: The paragraph's conclusion is fair, but both premises are stretched.
  - **Suggested rewrite**: "The settings of this system are shaped by experience, starting early. Rat pups whose mothers licked and groomed them more grow up with more glucocorticoid receptors in the hippocampus, stronger feedback, and smaller stress responses, a programming of the axis in the first days of life.[^65] And the response in the moment depends on company: women in an MRI scanner ... while holding their husband's hand.[^66]"

## Minor (imprecise wording)

- **Line 146 and footnote 83**: "Adrenaline does not enter the brain; McGaugh's laboratory showed that its effect is relayed by the vagus nerve".
  - **Wording**: Adrenaline crosses the blood-brain barrier poorly rather than not at all. Use "does not readily enter the brain".
  - **Citation**: The direct evidence for the vagal and NTS relay is Williams and McGaugh 1993 (Behav. Neurosci. 107:955, "Reversible lesions of the nucleus of the solitary tract attenuate the memory-modulating effects of posttraining epinephrine"), with later work by Williams and colleagues. McGaugh 2004 summarizes it, so the citation is acceptable. Adding Williams and McGaugh 1993 to footnote 83 would make the specific claim verifiable.
- **Line 20 and footnote 2**: "Walter Cannon gave the idea its modern name in the 1920s". This is correct, but footnote 2 cites only the 1929 and 1932 works. Add Cannon, W. B. (1926), "Physiological regulation of normal states: some tentative postulates concerning biological homeostatics", in A. Pettit (Ed.), *À Charles Richet: ses amis, ses collègues, ses élèves*, Paris: Éditions Médicales, p. 91, or leave as is.
- **Line 128**: "memory is best when the first is filled and the second not overwhelmed". de Kloet et al. describe optimal function at predominant MR occupancy with moderate, not minimal, GR activation, because GR activation is itself needed for consolidation. A safer phrasing is "when the first is occupied and the second moderately, not massively, engaged".
- **Line 26**: "Eighty years later". 1928 to 2011 is 83 years. This is fine as a round number; "More than eighty years later" is exact.
- **Figure 7.3**: "happy" is now at about 20 degrees; Russell's (1980) scaling puts it at about 8 degrees, close to the pleasant pole. This is labeled approximate and acceptable as it stands, but moving it next to "pleased" would match the source.
