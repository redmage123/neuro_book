## Overall assessment

The three round-1 Majors and nearly all the Minors are addressed. The new material has given the chapter what it lacked: people on the page in every section.

- **The Chapter 6 retelling is gone.** The slow-speed prior (line 106) is new to the book. The correction from real fog (Pretto) is handled honestly: "the simulator isolated the prior" tells the reader why a result that runs against intuition still counts. Chapter 6's light from above now appears only as a one-clause callback.
- **The added stories are the chapter's best new writing:**
  - The cricket batsmen (line 28). "They were not watching the ball so much as checking a prediction of it" is the chapter's thesis in a sentence a reader will remember.
  - Sea legs (line 98). "Anyone who has spent a few days at sea knows the feeling" is the first time in the chapter that the reader's own experience is the evidence. "the brain had built a prediction of the ground's motion and could not discard it at once" ties it neatly to Addams.
  - Stahl and Feigenson's infants (line 132). "The surprise told them where to look; the drive to explore was their own" makes the section's point visible.
  - The helicopter and the bucket (line 76) give the adaptive learning rate a picture.
- **The expert's additions strengthen the argument rather than burden it:**
  - Palmer and Chalk end on "That is this chapter's thesis in miniature: one strategy, with its implementation tuned to circumstance" (line 50).
  - Bowers and Davis (line 84) turn the chapter's skepticism on its own favored idea, which makes the free-energy section that follows read as even-handed rather than targeted.
  - The N400 hedge (line 66) and the Keller sentence that closes line 122 ("the evidence may depend on the kind of prediction that is violated") are fair to both sides.
- **The structure is repaired.** The three reasons now run in order: delay, then the batsmen, then the brain's own movements as the hardest case of delay, then economy, then regulation. The handover sits at the end of the AI section (line 150). The Central Lesson ends on "the strategy does not depend on the equation". The callbacks are now correct (Chapter 4 for place cells, Chapter 5 for synaptic scaling, Chapter 7 for Sterling).
- **The house style holds,** with no em dashes and no British spellings.

What keeps prose below 5 is that the additions have made four paragraphs long and multi-stranded (lines 76, 106, 124, 132, at 227 to 254 words each), and three sentences must be read twice (lines 62, 112, and 90). In one place, line 76, two studies are now in the wrong order. These are all Minor, and each has a fix below.

Reader interest is now 5. Every section has a scene, and the chapter moves from Craik's bicycle to the batsmen, the fly, the waterfall, the running mice, the helicopter, the voice-hearers, the ship, the cultured neurons, and the infants without a long stretch of abstraction.

Prose quality: 4/5. Reader interest: 5/5.

## Findings

### Major

None.

### Minor

- **Line 76 (250 words; studies out of order).** "In a later version of the task ... In a later study the same group found that the size of people's pupils ..." The pupil study (2012) predates the helicopter task (2014), and two consecutive "later"s blur the sequence.
  - Change the pupil sentence to begin "The same group also found that the size of people's pupils ...".
  - Move it before the helicopter sentence, so that the numbers task and its pupils come first and the helicopter follows as the later version.
  - Split before "Figure 12.5 shows such a learner".
- **Line 62 (contradiction in the reader's ear).** "People adjusted to changed rewards more readily than to changed sequences ...; they adjusted to both, and the authors concluded ..." **Rewrite the end:** "...the asymmetry a predictive map produces, since a map of what follows what must be relearned when the sequence changes but not when only the rewards do. Yet people did adjust to changed sequences, if less well, and the authors concluded that they combine such a map with deliberate planning." In the next sentence, "the hippocampal and entorhinal cortex" should be "the hippocampus and entorhinal cortex".
- **Line 106 (254 words, three strands).** Split before "Chapter 6's light from above". The second paragraph then runs from the light-from-above callback through the chicks to "Evolution sets the prior; experience can move it, within limits." In the first paragraph, "and the prior itself shifted toward faster speeds, changing how they saw the direction of faint moving lines" leaves the reader wondering why speed training changed direction. Use "and the prior itself shifted toward faster speeds, as a change in how they judged the direction of faint moving lines revealed."
- **Line 112 (stacked appositives).** "minimizing a quantity called variational free energy, a quantity the system can compute from its sensations and its own model, which is an upper bound on surprise, the improbability ..." **Rewrite:** "can be described as minimizing variational free energy, a quantity it can compute from its sensations and its own model and one that sets an upper bound on surprise, the improbability of its sensory states under that model."
- **Line 90.** The last sentence is now about 75 words. Split it: "Fitted to each person's responses, the model showed that the voice-hearers gave more weight to what they expected relative to what they heard. In brain scans, the conditioned hallucinations raised activity in the auditory regions that responded to real tones, along with a network including the insula and the anterior cingulate cortex that resembles the regions active when patients hallucinate."
- **Line 124 (246 words).** Split before "Brett Kagan and his colleagues". The Isomura study and its caveat then make one paragraph, and Pong with its critics the other. "Both studies show something real ..." stays at the end of the second.
- **Line 132 (227 words).** Split before "Chapter 11 described the most promising account of that drive". The infants stand alone, ending on "the drive to explore was their own", and the theory follows. The critics in the last sentence are the only unnamed group in the chapter. Use "Beren Millidge and his colleagues have argued that ..." (expert to confirm the lead author).

### Copyedits (optional; do not affect the score)

- **Line 12.** "The book is slight, and much of what this book has described" uses "book" twice for two different books. Use "Craik's book is slight, and much of what these chapters have described can be read as a footnote to it."
- **Line 66.** "the best-known evidence that readers anticipate the specific word to come" is vague at the point where the reader wants the detail. If the expert agrees, name it: "the best-known evidence that readers anticipate the word itself, a change in the wave at the article 'a' or 'an' that precedes an expected noun".
- **Line 98.** The sea-legs paragraph and the next one (line 100, "Prediction requires a model ...") meet without a bridge. Begin line 100 with "Sea legs are one timescale among many. Prediction requires a model, ..."
- **Line 50.** "Nearly every cell belonged to some small group whose spikes carried almost as much information about where the bar would be next as any code could" is accurate but dense. It may stand, but "almost as much information about the bar's next position as the bar's past made available" would read more easily (expert to confirm).

Counts: Major 0, Minor 7, Copyedit 4.
