## Overall assessment

Technical accuracy score: **5 / 5** (round 1: 4.5)

Every round 1 finding is resolved:

- **Inverted retina (line 112).** The functional case is now stated: photoreceptors against the tissue that feeds them and renews their pigment. That is consistent with Baden and Nilsson's abstract ("an inverted design actually offers many notable benefits"). The blind spot and the scattering layers are kept as real costs. This is fair to both sides.
- **Birds (line 106).** "About twice as many neurons as primate brains of the same mass, and a larger share in the forebrain" now matches Olkowicz et al.'s abstract. The 2025 convergence studies are attributed (line 108).
- **Callbacks.**
  - Prinz, Bucher and Marder 2004 replaces the nonexistent Chapter 5 passage (line 82) and is correctly described: millions of simulated versions of the crustacean stomatogastric circuit.
  - The squid sodium figure is reworded and sourced (line 34).
  - Dopamine vigor is cited to Niv et al. 2007.
- **Genome arithmetic (line 116).** The text now says "86 billion ... which Zador rounds to 100 billion" and "on the round numbers used here, the shortfall is a factor of more than half a million". It is now consistent with Figure 13.6 (589,000), and the superscript is fixed.
- **Levy and Baxter (line 26).** "0.025 to 0.05 in each interval of 2.5 milliseconds, about 10 to 20 spikes a second, against about 200" is internally consistent (0.025 to 0.05 per 2.5 ms is 10 to 20 Hz; 0.5 is 200 Hz), and you report it confirmed in the 2002 full text.
- **Harris et al. (line 32).** "In most cells ... came close to maximizing" now matches the paper (the maximum fell at the physiological conductance in six of ten cells and within 0.5 to 3 times it in three more), and "one in five" was confirmed.
- **Cornélio (line 88).** It is reworded without the unconfirmed hours.
- **Kuzawa (line 90).** The age-four peak in the ratios, plus total glucose use peaking at about five, is consistent with the paper's figures as reported.
- **Cordey et al. (line 94).** It is now precise: one study of 12 women, and the ceiling exceeded in a third of the studies reviewed. This matches the abstract.
- **AI section (lines 150 to 156).** All four refinements are in and accurate:
  - "largely a convergence", with the honest note on early mixtures of experts;
  - the chip restoring its signals as "the same answer the axon found";
  - "no counterpart in how large models are trained";
  - "Chapters 10 and 11".

The new material is also accurate:
- **Clune, Mouret and Lipson (2013), and Mengistu et al. (2016) (line 72).** Connection costs yield modular, more evolvable networks, which is consistent with Clune et al.'s abstract ("significantly more modular and more evolvable"); with nested problems, the same cost yields hierarchy. The hedge, "small, abstract networks ... not that it is what produced the brain's", is exactly right. The specific generation counts (about a twentieth as many) are as you report from the full text; I did not re-verify them.
- **The statistical constraint behind two memory systems (line 130).** This is the complementary-learning-systems argument stated correctly and in one sentence.
- **The cavefish callback in the Central Lesson (line 162).** It is apt.
