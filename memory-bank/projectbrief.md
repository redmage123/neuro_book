# Project Brief

## Overview
"How the Brain Learns" is a mechanistic neuroscience textbook written for technically literate readers without prior neuroscience training. The project includes a main textbook and a companion volume with student notes and laboratory exercises.

## Core Requirements
- Maintain parallel Word and PDF outputs for each chapter
- Keep main book and companion volume separate but aligned
- Support publisher workflows (Word) and print-ready artifacts (PDF)
- Preserve conceptual coherence and authorial voice

## Goals
- Explain **why** the brain works the way it does, not just what it does
- Ground all explanations in physical and biological mechanisms
- Distinguish molecular, cellular, circuit, and cognitive levels of explanation
- Treat biological constraints (noise, energy, slow adaptation) as central features

## Target Audience
- Software engineers
- Data scientists and ML practitioners
- Physicists and mathematicians
- Technologists interested in biological intelligence
- Educators seeking mechanistic neuroscience material

## Design Principles
1. **Mechanism over metaphor** — avoid hand-wavy analogies
2. **Cumulative structure** — chapters must be read sequentially
3. **Respect for levels of explanation** — don't conflate molecular with cognitive
4. **Technical respect for the reader** — dense but motivated prose
5. **Biological realism** — imperfection is a feature, not a bug
